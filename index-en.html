Our story

A tree that does not move

The cedar is the tree on the Lebanese flag: it grows slowly, it stands up to the wind, and it stays where it is. It seemed the right name for a restaurant born far from home.

Ahmad Fayad has been cooking here since 2022; his son Jamil looks after the room. The recipes do not come from a book — they are the family ones. Hommos made fresh every day, meat on the skewer over charcoal, warm pita that reaches the table before anything else — and at the end, nearly always, a mint tea on the house.

We chose the Darsena because it feels like a Lebanese harbour: boats, salt, people passing. The room is small and we like it that way — you can hear the next table, and the kitchen behind it. Which is why it is worth booking.

The fire does half the work

What guests tell us

Three things we hear often

Real Lebanese cooking

People who know Lebanon recognise the dishes: nothing softened for the Italian palate. The mezze arrive the way they are served in Beirut.

Good if you do not eat meat

Falafel, mtabbal, wara’ enab, fattoush: the vegetarian and vegan side is not an afterthought, it is half the menu.

Quiet, in the harbour

Off the tourist route. Music in the background, tables that are not crammed together, the pace of dinner rather than fast service.
